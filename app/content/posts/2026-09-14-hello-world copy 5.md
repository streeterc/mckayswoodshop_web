A router jig will cut a drawer's worth of dovetails in the time it takes to lay out one by hand. I still cut them by hand, on drawers where it matters — the ones at eye level, the ones a customer opens every day.

The tell isn't the joint's strength; a jig-cut dovetail holds fine. It's the pins: hand-cut pins are rarely identical width to width, and that irregularity is what reads as made, not manufactured. Uniform spacing is what a machine produces by default. Slightly uneven spacing is what four hundred years of cabinetmaking actually looked like.

Interior drawers, the ones nobody but the client will ever see the back of, get the jig. There's no version of craft that requires suffering where it doesn't show.

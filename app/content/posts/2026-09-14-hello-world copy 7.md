Walnut changes color faster than almost anything else on the rack — cut a fresh face and it's pale brown within the hour, then keeps deepening for weeks under light. This stool's seat came from a single 8/4 walnut board, saddled by hand with a travisher so it actually fits a person sitting on it, not just a flat disc with three holes drilled in it.

Legs are turned, splayed at eight degrees, and wedged through the seat from the top — the same joint that's held Windsor chairs together for three hundred years. No glue holds the wedge in place; the wood does, once it's dry and the wedge has driven the tenon tight against the sides of its hole.

Finished in hand-rubbed oil. Give it a year in daylight and it'll be a full shade darker than it left the shop.

Hand-rubbed oil is the default finish for anything that lives with a family — tables, stools, cabinet fronts. It's forgiving, easy to touch up with a rag and more oil, and it lets the wood darken naturally instead of sealing it under a film. The tradeoff is water rings and heat marks; oil doesn't stop either.

Conversion varnish goes on kitchen cabinetry and anything that will see steam, spills, or a dishwasher within arm's reach. It's sprayed, not wiped, and it cures hard enough to shrug off the things a kitchen actually does to a finish. It also changes the wood's color less than people expect — a sealer coat first keeps it from looking plastic.

If you're not sure which your piece needs, tell us where it's going and what it'll touch. That's usually enough to decide.

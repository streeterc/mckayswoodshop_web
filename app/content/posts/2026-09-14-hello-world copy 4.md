A dull chisel is more dangerous than a sharp one, because it needs more force, and force is what slips. Every chisel in the till gets the same routine: flatten the back first, once, and never touch it again unless it chips. After that it's the bevel — 1000, then 4000, then 8000 grit, a few strokes each, finished with a strop.

Five minutes, maybe less once the back is already flat. I do it before a cut needs it, not after, which means most chisels get touched up every few hours of use rather than waiting for a problem.

The stones live in a tray of water at the bench, not in a drawer. If sharpening isn't easier than reaching for a duller tool, it doesn't happen.

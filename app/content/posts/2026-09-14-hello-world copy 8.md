A face-frame cabinet has a half inch of forgiving lumber around every opening; a frameless cabinet doesn't. The box itself is the face, so every panel has to be cut, edge-banded and assembled to a tolerance that a frame would normally cover for you.

On a nineteen-foot run of frameless uppers and lowers, that meant checking every box for square before it left the shop — diagonal to diagonal, not just a framing square in the corner — because a box that's a sixteenth out of square telegraphs straight through to the door reveal once it's hung.

It's slower to build. It's also the look most people are actually asking for when they say they want something clean, even if they don't know the term for it yet.

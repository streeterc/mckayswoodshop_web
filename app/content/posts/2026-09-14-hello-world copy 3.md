Plans assume square walls. Old houses rarely have them. On a recent kitchen, the back wall ran 11/16 in out of true over nineteen feet — enough that a cabinet run built dead square would have left a wedge-shaped gap at one end, wider than a finger at the top.

We built the run square in the shop, the way it should be, then scribed each end panel to the actual wall on site: a compass set to the widest gap, a line drawn, a hand plane to take it down to the line. Slow work, and not glamorous, but it's the difference between a gap you can see and a joint you can't.

Every kitchen we install gets scribed this way. It's why the measure visit takes longer than people expect, and why the fit doesn't need caulk to hide it.
